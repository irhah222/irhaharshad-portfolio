AQS

Aqs means reflection, a fitting name for a story about water, and about what happens when we stop paying attention to it.

AQS hero image

The Challenge

AQS is a motion comic exploring Pakistan's water crisis through a speculative lens. It follows Shahmir, a man grappling with the loss of biodiversity in a world where the spirits of nature have turned against mankind — using speculative fiction to make an environmental crisis feel immediate rather than abstract. The hardest part was grounding a high-concept, speculative narrative in reality, which meant researching the water crisis in Balochistan directly. As a designer from Pakistan, this was personal: I wanted to advocate for a story that rarely gets told, and do it justice.

The Solution

I used the motion comic format to blend illustration, film direction, and design research into a single piece. Data from sources like the UNDP anchored the narrative in fact, while the visual storytelling made it emotionally immediate — the goal was something that felt both researched and felt.

Identity

The Mark

The logo needed a local touch, so I experimented with Urdu type and circular forms until I found a shape that could carry the story's themes on its own. It leans on circles to represent both the water cycle and the broader cycle of life — a quiet echo of the word the whole project is named for.

“Aqs” means reflection — the water theme was built into the name before it was built into a single frame.

The Outcome

As a capstone project, AQS set out to show that motion graphics, film direction, and design research together can shift perspective — proof that design can be advocacy, not just decoration. The final piece blends illustration, film direction, and research into a story that's both researched and felt.

Reflection

Proof that design can be advocacy: making the narrative both researched and felt was always the real goal of this project, more than any single frame of it.
