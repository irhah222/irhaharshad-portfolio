The Challenge

Imagin8v Park is a digital empathy engine for civic transformation, an attempt to make the wicked problem of the housing crisis legible to middle and high school students and the wider community, without flattening it into something simplistic. The goal was an accessible, interactive way into a policy issue that's usually discussed only in rooms most people never get into.

The Solution

The core of the experience is an interactive, scenario-based simulation built as a high-fidelity Figma prototype, letting users digitally visualize and customize community spaces while picking up civic and housing concepts along the way. A physical portal doorway, a laser-cut silhouette of Terry the ghost dog, and printed worksheets carried the world off the screen and into the exhibit space, so visitors walked through the story instead of just clicking through it.

Process

Five Phases, One Prototype

We moved from rough storyboards to a click-and-play prototype in five structured phases, checking in with partners like Adams Garden Park, CR8TV House, and Adam Carr along the way.

01) Storyboarding

Mapped how players move through the game and where they learn.

02) Cohesive Design System

Locked in color, type, icons, and character style.

03) Low-Fidelity Prototyping

Tested structure and mechanics with simple wireframes.

04) High-Fidelity Prototyping

Pushed toward final visuals, guided by partner feedback.

05) Final Draft

Refined and finalized the handoff-ready prototype.

Core Mechanic

Customizing the Community

Players start on a digital map of a neighborhood showing real, visible problems: locked buildings and lots waiting to be addressed. Clicking into one unlocks a task, and completing it reflects back on the map itself, so the neighborhood visibly improves as players learn, turning an abstract policy issue into something they can see themselves changing.

Cast

Meet the Guides

Five characters carry players through the story: Cherry and Barry, the two guides who work with players to improve their community and, in doing so, improve their own future too, plus Peach, Remy, and Granny Smith, who round out the cast of neighbors players meet along the way.

Special Mention

Terry

The dog who acts as the catalyst for starting the player’s journey in the game.

Real-World Inspiration

Cherry and Barry are grounded in real people: Sharon and Larry Adams, whose continued work at Adams Garden Park has been turning blighted lots into community space since 2020.

Narrative Design

The Story

A four-beat arc carries players from the problem to their own hand in the solution, told through Cherry and Barry as guides rather than a wall of text.

01

Dystopian Present

Players start in a neighborhood in decline, the wicked problem made visible and personal.

02

Meet Cherry & Barry

Players get pulled back in time and meet their guides, right where the decline began.

03

Community Tasks

Players take on civic engagement tasks, learning housing concepts by doing.

04

Thriving Present

Players return to a present day neighborhood shaped directly by their decisions.

Reflection

  • Learning directly from stakeholders like Sharon and Larry Adams made the whole project feel grounded, and worth the ambition of building a full game around it.
  • Taking that on in a single short semester meant picking up new techniques fast, and getting a much clearer sense of what’s actually realistic in that kind of timeframe.
  • The biggest lesson was how to get an idea moving no matter what stage it’s at, instead of waiting for the conditions to feel perfect before starting.
  • I’m still open to developing Imagin8v Park further, including building it out on other game engines.
