A go-to-market plan for a QR code platform that stays alive after it's printed — turning five customer interviews and a crowded, fragmented market into a $0.5M primary release small businesses would actually pay for.

The Problem

A printed QR code is a promise you can't take back. Once it's on 10,000 boxes or a stack of flyers, whatever link it points to is the link it points to — forever, or until you pay to reprint everything. Small businesses lean on QR codes constantly for packaging and promotions, but the tools underneath them haven't caught up: codes are static (tied to one fixed destination), rigid (the moment the link changes, the code breaks), and invisible (no insight beyond “someone, at some point, scanned this”).

Kirby Mann, a small e-commerce business owner, felt it firsthand: after her art store took off to 1,000+ orders a month, she misprinted an order of over 500 envelopes with a QR code pointing to the wrong link. Fixing it meant reprinting — expensive, slow, and entirely avoidable if the link underneath the code could have simply been swapped.

I ran discovery interviews with small business owners and marketing/events managers to understand how deep this problem actually ran, and to find out if anyone would pay to fix it.

Guiding Question. How might we give small businesses the same visibility and flexibility over their QR codes that a large enterprise marketing team takes for granted — without the cost, the headcount, or the technical overhead?

Insight 1

An intermediary link reads as a scam

Our entire redirect mechanism depends on routing scans through an intermediary link — and that's exactly what made one founder distrust his own packaging.

"...it kind of feels like, oh, is this a scam? Did I just get like a virus on my phone or something like that?"Interview: Daniel Adix, Founder & CEO, LiQure
Insight 2

Nobody notices when a code dies

Free QR tools expire codes silently. Materials stay up, the link goes dead underneath them, and no one finds out until a customer complains.

"Codes from last semester were still up even though they had stopped working entirely."Interview: Sandra Bradley, Tech Exploration Lab
Insight 3

Teams already pay for tracking — and lose it

One organization paid $15.99/month for location-based QR tracking, then dropped it to staffing cuts. He rated the resulting analytics-less experience 7 out of 10, losing points specifically for lost tracking and no logo customization.

"7/10 — losing points for lack of tracking and logo customization."Interview: Fadhel Alrabiah, TEO
Insight 4

Without a dashboard, people invent one

With no real analytics tool, one events team wired a link-shortener into their flyers purely to get a scan count — a number with no action attached to it. Another had a contractor build a one-off internal tool just to track kiosk transactions.

"It was nice to have a number, [but there was] no real action taken on the data."Interview: Tito, Multicultural Center
Key Pivot
"...it kind of feels like, oh, is this a scam? Did I just get like a virus on my phone or something like that?"
Daniel Adix, Founder & CEO, LiQure · Customer Interview

The premise of our entire product runs through an intermediary link — the exact thing that made a real customer question whether his own packaging was compromised. That single reaction reshaped the release plan: a branded, trust-building intermediary page before redirect became a top requirement, not a nice-to-have.

Who We're Building For

Two personas, two releases

"Honestly, I just need one convenient place to manage all my codes without it becoming a whole project."April O'Neil, Persona
Secondary Persona

Mark Majer, Digital Marketing Manager

28, an early adopter based in Cancun, MX, leading digital marketing for Cancun Events International. Deploys QR codes across flyers, signage, and promotions for events from small workshops to large festivals, and has to report campaign performance to sponsors and stakeholders with real data.

"I run campaigns across dozens of venues and channels. I need to know what is actually working — not guess."Mark Majer, Persona

The Competitive Landscape

The market isn't empty — it's crowded but fragmented. Nothing we found was both purpose-built and affordable for a non-technical small business.

Direct Competitors

QR Code Generator, Uniqode, QR.io

Full-featured, dynamic QR codes with built-in analytics — but high recurring pricing, usage-capped generation, and bulk tools that assume a technical team (API credits, not a CSV import).

Substitutes

Canva, Adobe Express, Google Analytics, Klaviyo

Already embedded in daily workflows and often free, so users stitch together a static QR code from one tool and traffic tracking from another — accepting the gaps rather than switching.

DIY Workarounds

Linktree click-counts, hand-edited forms, contractor tools

Teams route around the problem with whatever's cheapest and already familiar — editing a Google Form instead of a QR code, or a Linktree click count standing in for real analytics.

Our wedge: undercut on price, keep QR codes alive for life — even after a subscription lapses — and make bulk generation something a non-technical small business owner can actually do. Competitors win on feature depth; we win on usability and trust.

From Insight to Feature Set

What QRCO actually ships

Every feature category maps back to a specific piece of research, split across a Core capability, a cost-saver, a differentiator, and one delighter that costs little to build but changes how the product feels day to day.

Core Feature

Generate & Manage

Create print-ready QR codes in seconds and manage every code from one dashboard — no more juggling apps between generation and tracking.

Cost Saver

Link Redirection Update

Update a code's destination instantly, without reprinting. One wrong link costs hundreds in wasted materials — this is the feature that saved Kirby Mann's 500 envelopes.

Differentiator

Scan Analytics

See who's scanning, when, and for how long, with engagement-over-time graphs that show what's actually working — the visibility Fadhel and Tito were both improvising around.

Delighter

Clipboard Integration

Copy a high-definition QR code straight to your clipboard and paste it directly into Canva, Photoshop, or a packaging file — no extra export step.

Release Plan

Start narrow, win deeply, expand fast

Rather than build one release that tries to serve both personas, the plan splits into a primary release scoped tightly to small business owners' most urgent needs, with a secondary release layered on top for marketing and events managers.

Primary Release · Small Business Owners

Faster to market, lower cost, clearest ROI

Reprinting broken links is directly costly to this segment, and existing tools are either too basic or too expensive for them — the clearest, fastest win.

Top features: QR Generation & Dynamic Link Redirection, Clipboard Integration, Scan Metrics + Engagement-over-Time

Secondary Release · Marketing & Events Managers

Adds attribution, organization, and scale

This segment needs to know which venue, flyer, or channel is driving scans, and to manage many codes across simultaneous campaigns without chaos.

Adds on top: Campaign Tagging + Attribution, Bulk Export + Logo Customization, Analytics Export (PDF, CSV, Excel)

Outcome

Three of the release's most important features trace directly back to a specific moment in the research, not a guess about what a QR platform "should" have.

Interview-informed

Trust-Building Intermediary Page

Daniel Adix's "is this a scam?" reaction became a top requirement: a branded landing page before redirect, so the very mechanism our product relies on doesn't undercut the trust it's supposed to build.

Story-informed

Link Redirection Update

Kirby Mann's 500 misprinted envelopes are the exact scenario this feature exists to prevent — update the destination once, never reprint again.

Cross-segment

Scan Analytics & Engagement-Over-Time

Fadhel's lost $15.99/month tool, Sandra's dead codes still on the wall, and Tito's improvised click counter all point at the same gap: visibility that persists after launch day.

De-risking ran alongside the roadmap: a freemium entry point and onboarding measured in minutes, not hours, a beta test with 10+ active codes measuring how often Link Redirection prevents a reprint, and a blind test pitting QRCO's analytics against existing competitors on time-to-insight and feature comprehension.

Key Takeaways

  • Grounding a product plan in real interview quotes — not assumptions — surfaced a risk (the "is this a scam?" reaction) that a feature-parity checklist never would have.
  • A narrower, cheaper primary release beat a technically broader one on time-to-market, cost, and the speed of getting real feedback.
  • Freemium access and lifetime QR codes became the wedge against much larger, better-funded direct competitors.
  • The most credible pricing signal in the whole plan came from a customer already paying a competitor for less than we were proposing to offer.
